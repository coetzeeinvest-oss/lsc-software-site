How to choose school transport software in South Africa

Operators · 8 min read · Updated 26 November 2026
Back to blog

If you run a school transport business, you have probably noticed that most school transport software is built for someone else: school districts overseas routing hundreds of buses, or school administration systems with a transport tab bolted on. An independent operator in South Africa running a handful of vehicles across a few schools needs something different.

This is a checklist for choosing school transport software, whether you are comparing LiftMe with something else or still deciding if you need software at all. Full disclosure: we build LiftMe, so we have an opinion, and we say so where it matters.

The short answer

Choose software built for the school run, not generic fleet tracking. At a minimum it should handle recurring weekly routes, give drivers a simple phone app, keep parents updated without calls to your office, invoice in rand with VAT, and bill month to month so you are not locked in. Then check who sets it up, who supports you and how learner data is protected.

1. Is it built for school transport, or relabelled fleet software?

Generic fleet tools track vehicles for couriers and logistics. They do not know about learners, schools or parents. Ask to see the parent view and a recurring route in the demo. If all you are shown is a map of moving dots, you are looking at a fleet tracker, not school transport management software.

2. Does it handle recurring weekly routes?

The school run repeats: the same to-school and from-school routes, the same stops and the same drivers, Monday to Friday. Software that makes you rebuild routes every week does not remove admin, it just moves it. Look for routes you set once that repeat on their own, with a clear status for each trip from scheduled through to dropped off.

3. Can drivers use it on any phone?

Your drivers carry a mix of phones, and some will not be comfortable with anything complicated. Ask whether drivers need to download anything, which phones are supported, and how long a new driver needs to get started. A simple school transport app that works from a link, with nothing to install, removes most of that friction.

4. What do parents see?

For parents, the question is always the same: where is my child right now? Look for live vehicle status, notifications when something changes and a weekly view of each child's trips. Done well, this is what stops the calls to your phone. See how parents use it for what that looks like in practice.

5. Does it invoice the way South African operators bill?

Most operators price per school and per learner, bill monthly in rand, and charge VAT only if they are registered. Check that the software handles per-school pricing, builds invoices from the trips that actually happened and produces VAT-ready invoices. If invoicing is all you need today, our free school transport invoice template builds one in four steps.

6. Whose brand do parents see?

Parents trust the operator, not the software vendor. Check whether your logo, colours and web address appear on the parent portal and the app, or whether parents see the vendor's name instead. We cover why branding matters for a transport business in a separate article.

7. What does it cost, and what are you locked into?

Ask for the all-in monthly price. Then ask about setup fees, annual contracts, per-vehicle or per-learner charges that grow as you do, and extras such as notifications or tracking. For comparison, LiftMe is R499 a month for up to 2 vehicles, R1,299 for up to 8 and R2,799 for unlimited vehicles, billed month to month with no setup fee. The full breakdown is on the pricing page.

8. Who sets it up, and who answers the phone?

Moving your existing learners, routes and drivers into new software is the part that stalls most rollouts. Find out whether the provider sets it up for you or hands you a blank screen, how long it takes to go live, and whether support is in your timezone and reachable by phone or WhatsApp.

9. How is learner data protected?

A learner's name, address and daily travel times are personal information, and in South Africa that brings POPIA into play. Ask where the data is stored, who can see what, how one operator's data is kept separate from another's, and whether there is a written agreement covering liability and POPIA compliance before you go live. The kinds of school transport software you will find

A search for school transport software returns several different kinds of product. Knowing which one you are looking at saves a lot of time.

  • Generic fleet and GPS tracking. Good for knowing where vehicles are. No concept of learners, parents or billing per school.
  • School bus routing software for districts. Powerful route optimisation, but often designed and priced for large school districts abroad, which is usually more than a small South African operator needs.
  • School administration systems with a transport module. A transport add-on inside software built for school administrators, rather than for an independent operator's business.
  • Purpose-built operator software. Built for the transport business that runs the school run: routes, drivers, parents and invoicing in one place, priced for South African operators. This is the category LiftMe is in.

Red flags

  • The demo never shows what a parent sees.
  • You cannot get a clear monthly price without a long sales process.
  • A long contract with no way to start small.
  • Charges that grow with every learner you add.
  • No answer on POPIA or on how your data is kept separate from other operators.
  • Nobody local to call when a route goes wrong on a Monday morning.

Questions to ask on a demo

  • Can you show me a recurring route and what happens when I change one trip?
  • What does a parent see, and what does a driver see?
  • How are invoices built, and can I price per school?
  • What is the total monthly price for my number of vehicles, and is there a setup fee?
  • Can I cancel at the end of any month?
  • Who sets up my routes and drivers, and how long does it take?
  • How is learner data stored and protected, and can I see the agreement?
