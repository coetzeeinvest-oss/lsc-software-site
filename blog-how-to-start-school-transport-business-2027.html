How to start a school transport business in 2027: your countdown to Term 1

Operators · 3 min read
Back to blog

Public schools open for learners on 13 January 2027 on the official calendar (independent schools set their own dates), and many parents sort out next year's transport before the year ends. If you want to start a school transport business in 2027, or you already run one and want a calmer January, the next few months are your window. Here is a simple countdown to the first morning run.

Now to mid-November: numbers, route and paperwork

  • Start small and tight. Pick one or two schools in one area. A few full routes close together earn more than scattered seats across several suburbs.
  • Know your real monthly cost. Add finance, insurance, fuel, servicing and licensing, and remember the vehicle costs the same in the school holidays. Price by distance so far-away learners cover the extra kilometres.
  • Start the paperwork first. Find out which operating licence, Professional Driving Permit (PrDP), roadworthy certificate and police clearance apply to you, and how long each takes in your province. Ask a licensed broker for insurance that covers carrying learners for payment. Parents increasingly ask to see these documents, as our parent guide to choosing a school transport company explains.

Late November to mid-December: schools and parents

  • Meet the school offices before they close for the year. Ask about pickup zones, gate times and who may collect a learner. Schools remember the operators who make their lives easier.
  • Write a one-page parent agreement covering fees, holidays, notice periods and late payments. It is far easier to point to than to negotiate case by case.
  • Collect 2027 details in one place. Names, addresses, pickup points and emergency contacts belong in a system, not scattered across WhatsApp chats. Already operating? Confirm who is returning and check that every licence and certificate stays valid through 2027.

Mid-December to early January: systems and safety

  • Plan for the day something goes wrong. Line up a backup driver and a backup vehicle. Every child needs their own seat and seatbelt, so never carry more learners than seats.
  • Get your systems running before day one. This is where scholar transport software earns its keep. With LiftMe you build your recurring to-school and from-school routes once, assign drivers, and parents get live tracking plus automatic pickup and drop-off notifications, so you are not answering "where are you?" calls from the driver's seat. Distance-based pricing then feeds straight into VAT-ready invoices from completed trips.
  • Do a dry run. Drive every route at school-run time with the real stops, and fix the timing now, not in front of twenty parents.

The week before and the first week of Term 1: launch

  • Tell parents what to expect: pickup times, the driver's name and how to reach you. A parent portal on your own subdomain, with your logo and colours, makes a new business look established from week one. More on that in why branding matters for a transport business.
  • Watch the first week closely. Note every late pickup, route snag and parent question, and adjust fast. Early reliability is what turns first-week parents into the referrals that fill your next route.

Your first step this week

You do not need everything perfect by January, but the paperwork should be started and you should have a plan for routes, parents and billing. Pick the first item above and begin.
