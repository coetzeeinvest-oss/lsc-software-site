    Invoice checklist

    What to put on a school transport invoice

    A clear invoice gets paid faster. Parents should be able to see in a few seconds who is charging them, for which child, for what and by when to pay.

    This template already puts the right things in the right places. If you are VAT registered there are extra rules, so check SARS guidance or ask your accountant. This tool is a simple template, not tax or legal advice.

    • Your business details. Name, phone, email, address and logo.
    • An invoice number and dates. A unique number, the invoice date and the due date.
    • Who and which child. The parent or client, the learner names and the school.
    • The billing period. For example "February 2027".
    • Clear line items. A description, quantity and price for each fee.
    • The total due. With VAT shown separately if you charge it.
    • How to pay. Your banking details and a payment reference.
    Same invoice, different names

    School transport invoice or scholar transport invoice?

    In South Africa the same service goes by several names: school transport, scholar transport, learner transport, the school run or a school lift club. The invoice does the same job whichever name you use. It tells the parent which learner was transported, for which period, what it costs and how to pay.

    This template works for all of them, from a bakkie doing a handful of lifts to a fleet running several schools. If you want software to handle more than invoices, LiftMe is scholar transport software built for exactly these operators.

    • School transport. The everyday term, and the one most parents search for.
    • Scholar transport. The common South African term for carrying learners to and from school.
    • Learner transport. Often used by schools and in official documents.
    • School lift club. A shared lift between a few families, usually run informally.

    VAT and tax invoices

    Do school transport invoices need VAT?

    Only if you are VAT registered. Registering for VAT becomes compulsory when your taxable turnover passes R1 million in a 12-month period, and many small school transport operators are below that, so they issue ordinary invoices without VAT.

    If you are VAT registered, your invoice has to be a tax invoice. In this tool, tick I'm VAT registered in step 1 and add your VAT number. Your prices are treated as excluding VAT, and the invoice adds VAT at the rate you set, which starts at 15%. This is a simple template, not tax advice, so check SARS guidance or ask your accountant for the full rules.

    • The words "Tax invoice". The tool adds them when VAT is switched on.
    • Your details. Business name, address and VAT number. Add your address in step 1.
    • A number and a date. A unique invoice number and the invoice date.
    • What you charged for. A description, quantity and price for each item.
    • The VAT and the total. VAT shown separately from the subtotal.
